## `src/wn/src/input.rs` — the blame of a plan quotes the first line even when the reader already read it

**Settled** 2026-09-06 (branch issue-462, finding R-20260906T201251Z#N1)

`Chain::blame` closes the message of a plan with `The first line of it is
{line:?}.` The clause repeats text the reader already read whenever the reader
that refused the plan named a token that stands on that same line. The two cases
that do it:

- A fenced text no reader can take. The chain reader names the fence, and the
  clause names the fence again: `the run of claude printed a plan wn cannot
  read: "```json" is not an issue number. The first line of it is "```json".`
- Every refusal of the JSON reader. `JsonError::NotJson` carries
  `Snippet::new(text)` of the whole document, and a snippet of the whole document
  opens with the first line of it.

The finding is accurate. The clause earns its place on the third case, where the
reader named a token out of the middle of a text: `"an" is not an issue number.
The first line of it is "#277 an #278".` A reader who meets that message learns
the shape of what the run printed, which is what the clause is for.

It is not fixed, because the fix costs more than the repetition.

- **The only test that would drop the clause reads another reader's prose.**
  `blame` takes any error and knows nothing of which reader made it. To ask
  whether the clause repeats the error, `blame` must render the error to a string
  and search it for the snippet. That couples the message of the input to the
  wording of five readers, and every one of them is free to reword its own
  message. A rewording would then silently turn the clause back on or off.
- **A type that carried the answer would widen five errors.** `blame` could take
  a reader error that states whether its own snippet is the first line of the
  text. That is a new field on `ChainError`, `JsonError`, `PlanError`,
  `GraphError`, and every error added later, to decide the wording of one clause.
- **The defect is eight characters of repetition on a failure path.** The message
  already names the run, carries the reason, and names no clipboard, which is the
  whole of what issue #459 and issue #462 asked for.

The boundary: this settles the clause as it stands, for the wording of any
reader. A finding that the clause is *wrong* — that it quotes a line that is not
the first line, or that it quotes a text the run did not print — is a different
finding and is not settled here.
