## `src/msgs/scripts/dev-utils.ts` — `parsePort` makes the branded `Port` with `as`

**Settled** 2026-11-25 (branch msgs, finding R-20261125T194029Z#N1)

The finding is accurate. `parsePort` ends with `return port as Port`, and
`dev-utils.ts` is production code, not a test.

`parsePort` is the constructor of the brand. It is the one function that makes
a `Port`, and it makes one only after a check of the text and of the range. A
brand on a `number` has no form at runtime, thus each way to make one holds a
claim that the compiler does not prove. A type predicate (`n is Port`) is the
same unchecked claim in a different spelling. The tests in
`dev-utils.test.ts` hold the check: they accept `"4123\n"`, and they refuse
`""`, `abc`, `0`, `70000`, `12 34`, and `-5`.

The cost of a fix: a different spelling of the same claim, which a new review
round must read. It removes no risk.

The boundary: the one `as` that makes a `Port` in `parsePort`, after its check,
is this decision. These changes reopen it:

- A second site in production code makes a `Port` with `as`.
- `parsePort` returns a `Port` before its check, or without it.
