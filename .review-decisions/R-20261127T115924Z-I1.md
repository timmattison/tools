## `src/wdid/src/command_words.rs` `is_pattern` — the rarer brace and glob forms stay out of the pattern rule

**Settled** 2026-11-27 (branch issue-729, finding R-20261127T115924Z#I1)

The finding was correct. Commits fbb1999 and 5ec5c53 fixed the two forms
that it named: a brace pair that expands nothing before a pattern
(`{x}{a,b}`), and a pattern that a quoted piece divides (`{a,"b"}`). Commit
a75e571 fixed a third form, a line join (finding R-20261127T120930Z#I1). This
record settles each other rare form of the same rule.

`is_pattern` is a heuristic, not a parser of globs. It knows the forms that a
startup file usually holds: `*`, `?`, `[` before `]`, a `{` and the first `}`
after it with `,` or `..` between them, and a first `=`. It does not read the
options of the shell, and it does not do a check of a whole brace sequence.
These words show the gap:

- `[ab"]"` gives the literal `[ab]`, as bash does. zsh reads a pattern.
- `@(a|b).c` (bash `extglob`) and `{ab}` (zsh `BRACE_CCL`) give a literal.
  Under those options, the shell expands them.
- `{a..}` gives an expansion, but bash and zsh print `{a..}`.

A missed form only keeps a word as a literal, and an extra form only keeps a
word as an expansion. No command runs, and no definition changes. The worst
result is a false `SameExpansion` finding, for example
`alias a='echo {ab}'` and `alias b="echo '{ab}'"` under `BRACE_CCL`, or a
missed one. Each review round of this branch found one more form, and each fix
drew one more round. A parser of each glob and brace grammar of two shells,
with their options, costs much more than a rare false finding.

The boundary: the forms that `is_pattern` knows, also when a quoted piece, a
line join, or a brace pair that expands nothing divides them, are in scope. A
miss there is a defect, and it reopens this decision. Each other form is
settled here: a form that depends on a shell option, an extended glob, the
check of a brace sequence, a quoted end of a bracket, and each extra form. A
review that finds a new form of that kind gives a new argument, not a new
occurrence. A real alias or function of a startup file that gives a false
finding because of such a form is a new occurrence.
