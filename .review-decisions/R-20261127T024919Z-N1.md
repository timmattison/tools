## `src/nle/src/filters/backspace.rs` `erase_char` — under type-ahead during a slow echo, the filter removes a key and keeps its echo

**Settled** 2026-11-27 (branch issue-152, finding R-20261127T024919Z#N1).
**Widened** 2026-11-27 (branch issue-152, finding R-20261127T055919Z#N1).

The findings are accurate. Type-ahead during a slow echo can put the echo of a
key after the next key. The filter then removes a key and keeps its echo. The
screen at the end is correct, but the viewer sees one moment that the typist
did not type. Two cases are known.

**Double type-ahead** (finding R-20261127T024919Z#N1). The match path of
`erase_char` removes a typed key and the Backspace after it, but keeps their
echoes. An example is this order of events: in `h`, out `h`, in `r`, in DEL,
in `e`, out `r`, out `"\b \b"`, out `e`. The units of `r` and DEL have no
output, thus the screen after the DEL unit is the screen before `r`. The filter
removes the inputs `r` and DEL and cuts 0.55 s. The echoes `r` and `"\b \b"`
stay in the unit of `e`. Thus the viewer sees the typo for a moment, with no
key press.

**Single type-ahead with an unrelated output** (finding
R-20261127T055919Z#N1). `erase_without_typed_key` removes a Backspace whose
unit has output and changes nothing on the screen. An example is this order of
events: in `h`, out `h`, in `r`, out `r`, in DEL, out BEL, in `e`,
out `"\b \b"`, out `e`. The screen after the DEL unit is `hr`, not the screen
before `r`, thus the match path does not apply. The BEL changes nothing, thus
the filter removes DEL and BEL. The late echo `"\b \b"` stays in the unit of
`e`. Thus the viewer sees `r` go with no Backspace key.

The code and the docs agree. The module doc of `backspace.rs` (the third item
of "The limits") and the limits of the filter in `README.md` name both cases.
Tim decided to keep the behavior as documented, and to change no code
(decisions H-152-2 and H-152-3 on issue #152).

**The fix costs more than the defect.** There are two possible fixes:

- A lookahead through `SpanContext` for a later echo of the removed keys. The
  filter must then read the output events after the unit, and match them to an
  earlier key. That is a new mechanism for a rare case. Both cases need a slow
  echo, and the second case also needs an unrelated output inside the erase
  unit before its late echo. Tim refused this fix for both cases.
- For double type-ahead only: keep each Backspace unit with no output also on
  the match path. That makes the filter safe under double type-ahead, but then
  a typo at a prompt that echoes nothing stays, and
  `with_no_echo_a_backspace_erases_the_newest_typed_key` changes. That cost
  falls on a common case to fix a rare one. This fix does not help the second
  case, because the unit of its Backspace has output.

**The boundary.** This record settles only the case of type-ahead during a slow
echo: the filter removes a key, and the echo of that key comes after a later
key and stays. Both cases above are inside this boundary. These occurrences
reopen it:

- the same symptom (an input removed, its echo kept) in a case that has no
  type-ahead during a slow echo
- a case of type-ahead in which the screen at the end of the span is not
  correct
- a change to the match path of `erase_char`, to `erase_without_typed_key`, or
  to the limit text in `backspace.rs` or in `README.md`, that makes the code
  and the docs disagree
- a recording from real use in which this order is not rare
