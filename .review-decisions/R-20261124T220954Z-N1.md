## `src/faulte/src/stream.rs:486` — `next_event` gives `None` for a closed channel, the same as for a timeout

**Settled** 2026-11-24 (branch issue-669, finding R-20261124T220954Z#N1)

The finding is accurate. `TopStream::next_event` calls `recv_timeout(wait).ok()`,
thus `RecvTimeoutError::Disconnected` and a timeout both give `None`. The
reader thread (`read_stream`) sends `Ended` on each exit that it takes. If the
reader thread panics, it drops the sender with no `Ended`. Then
`recv_timeout` returns at once on each call, and the loop of `Sampler::read`
(`sampler.rs:217-222`) continues with no wait. One core then runs at 100%
until the stop flag is set, and no gap tells the person that the record
stopped.

No path to a panic in `read_stream` is known. Its one slice of the buffer is
`buffer.get(..count).unwrap_or_default()`, and each send and each read
handles its error. The defect thus needs a defect in the reader first.

The fix is new code: a change of the contract of `FaultStream::next_event`
or of `TopStream`, a test with a fake stream that closes its channel, and a
change in the loop of `Sampler::read`. The round that found it was
record-only. New code starts one more review round, and that round costs
more than a guard against a panic that no path reaches.

The boundary: this settles the closed channel with no known cause. A path
that makes `read_stream` panic, or a `FaultStream` that gives `None` with no
wait for a reason other than a closed channel, is a new finding.
