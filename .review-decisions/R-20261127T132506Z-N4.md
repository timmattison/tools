## `src/nle/src/filters/keystroke_hud/keys.rs` — a pending string start has no bound on its total span

**Settled** 2026-11-27 (branch issue-161, finding R-20261127T132506Z#N4)

The finding is correct. `feed` gives a pending sequence the time of the last
event that joins it. After `ESC ]`, `ESC P` or `ESC _` with no terminator, each
key that comes within `SEQUENCE_TIMEOUT` (50 ms) of the key before it joins the
pending string. The keys come out after the first gap of more than 50 ms, all
with the time of the last event. Thus the HUD shows them late, by the length of
the burst. A held key that repeats faster than 50 ms after Alt+] is the most
probable case.

The HUD loses no key, and it shows each one in the correct order. The case
needs Alt+], Alt+P or Alt+_ and then a burst of keys with no gap of 50 ms. The
reset of the time is necessary, because a long OSC reaches the recording over
many reads of 4096 bytes. A bound on the total span, or on the length, is a new
rule in the state machine of `feed`, and it must keep that long OSC whole. This
round of the review loop is record-only. A code change is new code that a
review must read, and that review can start a new fix round.

Boundary: a change to the join rule of `feed` reopens this decision. A real
recording where the HUD shows the keys after Alt+], Alt+P or Alt+_ late
reopens it too. A new argument about a bound does not.
