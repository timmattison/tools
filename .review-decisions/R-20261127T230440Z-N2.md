## `src/nle/src/filters/keystroke_hud/timeline.rs` — rule 2 does not say that a press at the time of an `Enter` opens the entry again

**Settled** 2026-11-27 (branch issue-161, finding R-20261127T230440Z#N2)

The finding is correct. d4829488 lets a press at `last` join a closed entry,
and `Timeline::new` then sets `open` from the key that joined. Thus `Enter`
at 1.0, `x` at 1.0, and `y` at 1.1 with a duration of 1.0 give one box,
`Enter x y`. Rule 2 of the doc of `Timeline` says that `Enter` closes the
entry, and it does not say that a join at `last` opens the entry again. No
test of `timeline/tests.rs` or of `keystroke_hud/tests.rs` holds the case.

The code is correct, as the finding says. One input event holds `Enter` and
`x`, so `x` is on the new command line, and `y` then adds to that line. The
gap is one sentence of the doc and one test that pins behavior that is
already correct.

The fix is prose and a new test. A change of the doc and of the tests is new
text that a fresh review must read, and the loop then starts one more fix
round. This round of the review loop is record-only. The finding
R-20261127T224006Z#N1 reports the same gap with the input `ls`, `Enter`, `pwd`
at 1.0 and `x` at 1.1, and its record states the same decision.

Boundary: a change to the join rule of `Timeline::new`, or to how it sets
`open`, reopens this decision. A change that keeps the entry closed after such
a join, and that passes the tests, reopens it too. A new argument for the
sentence or the test does not.
