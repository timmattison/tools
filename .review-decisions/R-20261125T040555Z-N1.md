## `src/faulte/src/history.rs:817` — `History::add` evicts after it compacts the new frame

**Settled** 2026-11-25 (branch issue-669, finding R-20261125T040555Z#N1)

The finding is accurate. `History::add` compacts the new frame
(`history.rs:803`), and `compact` acquires the text of each new identity.
Only then does the loop at `history.rs:817` evict the oldest entries. During
one call of `add`, the history thus holds its old entries and the whole new
frame. The budget test reads the live bytes after each `add`, so it does not
see that moment.

The excess has a limit that the tool already pays. `add` takes the sampled
frame by value, and each row of that frame holds its full command text.
`compact` stores a cut copy of a command only when the identity is new or
its cut text changed (`set_text`, which drops the old copy). The cut is never
longer than the command. The loop of `compact` consumes the rows, so each row
drops its full text after `acquire` copies the cut. The text in memory during
`add` is thus never more than at the moment of the call, plus one command of
at most `STORED_COMMAND_BYTES`. The compact rows and the index entries of the
new frame come on top, at some tens of bytes for each row. The 1.5 times of
the finding needs a sample that alone holds half of the budget in command
text, and the sampler holds that sample before the call.

The fix evicts before the compaction, with an estimate of the size of the new
frame. The estimate needs a pre-pass over the rows that copies the identity
lookup and the cut of `compact`. It is also wrong when the eviction releases
an identity that the new frame then acquires again. That code and its test
cost more than an excess that the sample already holds.

The boundary: this settles the transient of one call of `add`, with a size
up to the text of the sample that the call takes. A history that holds more
than its budget after `add` returns, or an excess that is larger than the
sample, is a new finding.
